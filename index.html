Priority language

Order: urgent, miai, claim, plus, equal, minus. Claimeven and claimodd share one claim level. Any level exposing two cells at once is an invalid diagram; uniqueness is part of the language. Miai is ignored unless exactly one is exposed. An empty cell is claimeven, written as a dot on disk.

Verification

What is checked

Every edit is re-verified automatically. Red follows the diagram and every legal Yellow reply is searched. Red must make four in a row on every line. A draw, a Yellow win, or a position where the diagram names no unique move all count as failures. Simplify blanks every marker that is not load-bearing. It is worked out with the verification, so the button says how many it would clear before you press it, and a candidate from the search can be reduced before you keep it.

The two searches

Fill undecided cells completes the diagram on screen. Every marker you have set is held fixed and only the undecided cells are searched; erase a cell (right-click) to have it re-derived. Each SAT model is a candidate, the exhaustive verifier turns every losing line into a clause, and only a diagram that passes the full check is offered.

It completes diagrams; it does not solve open roots. Four free cells finish in about 14 candidates, ten in about 120, twelve in about a thousand. A bare root with everything free ran 92,000 candidates without converging, and a faster solver would not change that: the limit is how little each counterexample rules out, not throughput. Because your markers bound the search, an UNSAT here means no completion of those markers wins, not that the root is impossible.

Solve whole root is the native engine, and it ignores the markers on screen: it solves from the position and replaces the whole diagram if it finds one. Its UNSAT is about the root itself, across the entire strict language. Neither verdict has been independently audited.
